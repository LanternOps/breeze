###### Observation needing a decision: software-MFT picture quality after a burst on a near-static screen
- **What was seen (verified):**
  - Before change 1, the viewer was sharp: taskbar clock readable, 12:24.
  - After the 7-frame Esc burst, the delivered picture was heavily blocky, and the taskbar clock and date were unreadable.
  - It stayed that way: at +60 s, at +80 s, and after the later single-frame change. That change was delivered, but its frame was blocky too.
  - On the OpenH264 control, the same Start-open, static, Esc step left a sharp picture, with the clock readable at 12:36.
- **Caveat (not like-for-like):** in the MFT run the desktop showed the Windows wallpaper. That session started at the sign-in screen, and the wallpaper was not suppressed after the mid-session sign-in. The control started on the signed-in desktop, so its wallpaper was suppressed to black. The taskbar region is the same in both.
- **Likely cause (inferred, not established):** the MFT runs CBR at about 2.7 Mbps. A large change costs more than one frame's budget, so quality drops. Because capture only produces a frame when the screen changes, there are no later frames in which quality can recover. OpenH264 is not affected in the same way here.
- **Impact:** only on the new fallback path (no GPU encoder and OpenH264 unavailable). Before 0.128.0 such a host refused the session, so this is not a regression. The change itself is delivered within about 1–2 s and the frames are current; what suffers is legibility until the screen changes again.
- **Not in the stated PASS criteria** (delivery within about 2 s, clocks match, `encoded` tracks `captured`, no storm), all of which were met. Clocks matched at +2 s; afterwards the viewer clock was unreadable.
- **Suggested follow-up:** an issue for a quality refresh, such as a refinement or keyframe after a burst on an idle screen, on the software-MFT path.

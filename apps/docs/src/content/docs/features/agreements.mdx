Three things in Breeze are easy to confuse, so it is worth naming them once: an **agreement template** is the legal document you keep on file -- your MSA or standard terms. A **signed agreement** is the frozen copy a customer signed when they accepted a quote carrying that template. A **contract** is the recurring biller that the same accepted quote creates. The signed agreement is filed against the contract it governs, and both live one click apart.

Open **Agreements** from the sidebar. It has two tabs: **Agreement templates** (the library) and **Signed agreements** (everything your customers have signed).

## Agreement templates

An agreement template is owned either by your whole partner (usable on any customer's quotes) or by a single organization, chosen when you create it.

### Creating an agreement template

<Steps>
1. Go to **Agreements → Agreement templates** and click **New agreement template**.
2. Name it and choose its owner: your **partner** (available to every organization) or one **organization** (available only there).
3. Add a version -- either **author** the document as rich text directly in Breeze, or **upload a PDF** (up to 10MB). An uploaded file must be a readable, unencrypted PDF.
4. **Publish** the version once it's ready to use on quotes.
</Steps>

A template's own page shows what it is used by -- how many quotes carry it and how many signed agreements came from it -- so you can see the blast radius before you archive one.

### Versions

Each template can have multiple versions. A new version starts as a **draft** you can keep editing; **publishing** it locks the content and assigns it a version number -- published versions are immutable, so a template already attached to a quote never changes underneath the customer. To revise a published template, add another version and publish that one; existing quotes stay pinned to whichever version they were attached with (see [Contract blocks](/features/quotes/#contract-blocks) in the quote editor).

**Archive** a template to stop it from being used on new quotes. Archiving doesn't touch versions already attached to existing quotes or the signed agreements generated from them.

<Aside type="note" title="Templates attach to drafts, not to accepted quotes">
  You add an agreement template to a quote while the quote is still being built. Acceptance is what freezes it: the customer sees the exact version that was attached, and accepting the quote turns that version into a signed agreement.
</Aside>

### Variables

An authored template can include `{{variable}}` placeholders that Breeze fills in per quote when the block is rendered or sent. Each placeholder is either:

- **Auto** -- filled automatically from the quote, with no data entry required. The built-in auto variables are the customer's name and address, your organization's (seller) name, the quote number and title, the one-time/monthly/annual/total amounts, and the effective and expiry dates.
- **Manual** -- anything else. The technician attaching the template to a quote fills these in by hand (for example, a scope note or a specific term).

Publishing a version scans its body for `{{variable}}` tokens and classifies each one automatically -- a name that matches a built-in auto variable is treated as auto, everything else as manual.

## Signed agreements

**Agreements → Signed agreements** lists every agreement your customers have signed, newest first, with the template and version they signed, the organization, the signer, the date, the quote they accepted, and the contract the agreement is filed against. Each row can be downloaded as the PDF the customer saw.

An agreement ends up **unlinked** when the accepting quote produced no contract, or produced one later -- the signed copy exists, but nothing points at it from the billing side. Turn on the **Unlinked only** chip to triage exactly those, and use **Link** on a row to file it against the right contract. The page shows everything by default: it is your inventory, not a to-do list.

Two other places show the same list, scoped:

- A **contract's** page has a **Signed agreements** section listing what was signed for that contract, and its header names the agreement the contract sits under.
- An **organization's record** has an **Agreements** section under Contracts & Billing, listing everything that customer has signed.

### Send an agreement for signature

You do not need a quote to get an agreement signed. **Send for signature** on **Agreements → Signed agreements** (or from a contract's **Signed agreements** section, which pre-fills the organization and the contract) emails a customer a link to review and sign a template on its own.

<Steps>
1. Click **Send for signature** and pick the **organization** and the **agreement template**. Only a template's published version can be sent; the request is pinned to that version, so later edits never change what the customer sees.
2. Optionally choose a **contract** to file the signed agreement against, add the **recipients** (pre-filled from the organization's billing contact), an optional **cover message**, and an **expiry** (by default 30 days from now, or the number of days set under **Settings → Billing → Agreements**). Fill in any manual **Agreement details** the template needs -- a template that uses quote variables (the quote number, totals, expiry date) has no quote to fill them from, so the dialog asks you to type those values in.
3. Check the **preview**: it is exactly the document the customer will see. Breeze won't send while a variable is still unresolved, so a blank value never ends up in a legal record.
4. Click **Send**. Every recipient gets an email with the signing link.
</Steps>

The customer opens the link -- no login -- reads the agreement, types their name and email, and **signs** or **declines** (a reason is optional). You get an email when they answer. A signed request becomes a signed agreement like any other: it appears in the list, is filed against the contract you chose, and both you and the customer get the **certified copy** described below.

Pending requests live under **Awaiting signature**, and **Declined / voided / expired** holds the ones that did not end in a signature. Each pending row has three actions:

- **Remind** re-sends the **same** link to the recipients and counts the reminder ("Reminded 2 times"). It works while the request is awaiting signature or has been opened but not signed. If the email can't go out, Breeze says so and the reminder is still recorded -- use **Copy link** to share it yourself.
- **Copy link** copies the signing link so you can send it another way.
- **Void** withdraws the request. The link stops working immediately, and a signed agreement can't be voided.

A request **expires** at its expiry time: the link refuses signatures from that moment, and within about fifteen minutes the request moves to **Declined / voided / expired** as **Expired**. Nothing is deleted -- send a new request to ask again.

### Default expiry and automatic reminders

**Settings → Billing → Agreements** holds two defaults for every agreement request your partner sends. It is the only place they are set; there is no per-organization override.

- **Default link expiry (days)** -- how long a new request's signing link lasts when the sender does not pick a date (1-365, 30 unless you change it). The Send dialog pre-fills the expiry from it and names where it came from; the sender can still pick any other date for that one request. Changing the setting never moves the expiry of a request that already exists.
- **Send automatic reminders** -- off unless you turn it on. When on, Breeze re-sends the **same** signing link to the recipients every **N** days (1-30) after the request was sent or last reminded, and stops after the number of reminders you choose (1-10). Manual **Remind** clicks count toward that limit and restart the wait. Reminders never go to a request that is signed, declined, voided or expired, nor to an organization that is suspended or archived. Turning reminders off stops them for every request at once.

Automatic reminders go out from an hourly background job (`agreementRequestAutoReminder`), so one can arrive up to an hour after it is due. Each one is audited as `billing.agreement.reminded` with `automatic: true`. If the email fails, the reminder still counts and the next one waits a full interval.

Changing these settings needs the Billing settings permission (`invoices:write` with full partner organization access) **and** `agreements:send`.

<Aside type="note" title="What the customer receives">
  When the customer signs, they are emailed a copy. By default that is the **certified copy**: the agreement as signed, plus a final page recording who signed, when, how, and the document's fingerprint (SHA-256). In the signed agreements list, **Download PDF** gives the certified copy and **Original** gives the file exactly as it was filed.
</Aside>

### Record signed agreement

Not every agreement arrives through a quote or a signing link. When a customer signed your MSA elsewhere -- in DocuSign, on paper, or by replying to an email -- file it with **Record signed agreement** on **Agreements → Signed agreements** (v0.124.0+). The dialog asks for the organization, the agreement template, an optional contract to file it against, **How it was agreed** (verbally or by phone, by email, a signed document, a purchase order, or other), a **Reference** a reviewer could follow -- a PO number, an email and date, or a ticket -- **Who agreed** and their email, the **Date signed**, and an optional **Signed copy** (PDF, PNG or JPEG, up to 10 MB). With a signed copy attached, that file is what gets filed; without one, Breeze files the template version and asks you to fill in any manual **Agreement details** it needs. The recorded agreement carries that evidence -- method, reference, signer and date -- and the list shows it as "recorded by MSP" with the method, as opposed to "via quote". Recording cannot be undone: a recorded agreement cannot be deleted afterwards.

The certified copy of a recorded agreement words its evidence page as *recorded by* your technician *as stated by the client* -- it never claims the client signed it themselves. If you attach a scan or photo, the certified copy is a PDF made from it plus that evidence page, and the **original** download returns your upload untouched.

<Aside type="caution" title="Known gap: templates that use quote variables">
  Without an attached file, a template whose body uses the quote variables stops at "Provide a value for: quote.number", because there is no quote to fill them from. Attach the signed copy instead (#9296).
</Aside>

## Permissions

| Action | Permission |
|---|---|
| View agreement templates and signed agreements | `agreements:read` |
| Create, edit, publish and archive templates; link a signed agreement to a contract | `agreements:write` |
| Send an agreement for signature, remind, copy its link, void it, record one signed elsewhere | `agreements:send` |

Managing partner-wide templates additionally requires full partner organization access.

**Record signed agreement** needs `agreements:send` from the release after v0.124.0 (it used `agreements:write` before). On upgrade, every role that holds `agreements:write` -- built-in or custom -- is granted `agreements:send` automatically, so nobody loses access; remove it from a role afterwards if that role should edit templates but not send or record agreements.

These are **separate** from contract permissions, deliberately: a technician who needs to pull up a customer's signed MSA does not need contract billing authority, and a billing clerk who runs recurring contracts does not need to author legal terms. Assign them through **Settings → Users & Roles**.
